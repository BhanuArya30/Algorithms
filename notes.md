7 tips to succeed in your coding interview:
1. Do not jump straight into coding - take the first 3-5 minutes to understand the problem and ask clarification questions. Ensure you fully understand what is expected of you to solve by the engineers before coding anything. Discuss the advantages and disadvantages of different solutions you could create and verbally confirm to the interviewer which solution you will create and the reasons why.
2. Think Out Loud - walk the interviewer through step by step what you're doing. We will want to know how you approach and troubleshoot problems. If the interviewer gives you hints to improve your code, take them and run with the. It is good to adjust and work through the problems with the interviewer to show your thought process and problem solving ability. You should be walking them through step by step what you're coding - think of this as a pair programming exercise you conduct with a junior engineer.
3. Think about your algorithm(s) - sorting, divide-and-conquer, recursion..), including the complexity and approximate runtime. Then figure out your data structure (array, stack/queue, hashset/hashmap/hashtable/dictionary, tree/binary tree, heap, graph, bloom filter etc) and implement. Listen to the interviewer if they provide you with a hint to use something else and adapt your approach.
4. Importantly, after you have finished writing your code, run through it verbally with your interviewer. This is really important. Does it really do what you think it does? Make sure to read what is there, not what you think is there.
5. Test your code, put it in an input to see hat happens. We're looking for you to find the bugs yourself and fix anything that comes up. Don't rely on the interviewer to nudge you on this - proactively test your code and fix your bugs!
6. Restate the complexity. Is it the same, or different to your initial thinking based on what you have actually coded up? Make sure you're thinking about both space and time complexity.
7. Optimise. Proactively suggest ways to optimise to the interviewer and get their feedback to ensure what you're trying ot do is not overly complex and is correct, then code it up.
